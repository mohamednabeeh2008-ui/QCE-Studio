# R-07 — Multi-Subject Resolution

Status: LOCKED

## Governing Decision
Journey counting is prohibited before Formation Subject resolution.

EVENT FAMILY → SUBJECT INVENTORY → ROLE CLASSIFICATION → ACTION/CHANGE ATTRIBUTION → PRIMARY SUBJECT TEST → COMPOSITE SUBJECT TEST → COUNTER-JOURNEY TEST → READER-PROJECTION EXCLUSION → JOURNEY ELIGIBILITY → LOCK

## Core Rule
CHARACTER PRESENCE ≠ FORMATION SUBJECTHOOD. A famous or event-central character cannot receive another participant's actions, pressures, corrections, or transitions.

## Subject Roles
PRIMARY_FORMATION_SUBJECT; SECONDARY_FORMATION_SUBJECT; COMPOSITE_FORMATION_SUBJECT; COUNTER_JOURNEY_SUBJECT; PRESERVED_SUBJECT; SUPPORTING_SUBJECT; EVENT_CENTRAL_NOT_FORMATION_PRIMARY; PARTICIPANT_ONLY; UNRESOLVED.

## Primary Subject Gate
A subject-specific arc requires: initial position → trigger/exposure → subject response → pressure/correction/disclosure → subject-specific outcome.

## Composite Subject Gate
Composite subjecthood requires genuinely shared task or pressure, coordinated action, shared transition, and inability to represent the participants accurately as parallel arcs. Materially different responses prohibit automatic composition.

## Shared Trial Rule
ONE SHARED EVENT ≠ ONE SHARED JOURNEY. Father, son, leader, community, and opponent may each require independent trajectories.

## Group Rule
A named group is not automatically one subject. Test response, action, and closure uniformity and preserve explicit subgroups.

## Counter-Journey Rule
Opponents may possess valid trajectories, including recognition followed by reversal or opposition followed by recognition and steadfastness.

## Preserved Subject Rule
Being saved or protected does not automatically create a Journey. The person receiving instruction, bearing uncertainty, responding, and changing may be the true Formation Subject.

## Reader Rule
READER ACTIVATION ≠ EVENT SUBJECT. Reader effects remain outside the historical subject model.

## Journey Eligibility
No Journey ID is minted before both subject verdict and Event Family boundary verdict are locked.

## Evidence Rule
Canonical subject assignment must be defensible at E0/E1. E2 may support a labeled Formation hypothesis; E3 cannot determine subject identity.

## Conflict Resolution
Compare SINGLE-SUBJECT and MULTI-SUBJECT models by action attribution, transition ownership, correction ownership, closure ownership, unsupported transfer count, and information loss. Choose the model with less transfer and less loss.
